Zeyu Wang

Visual & Interaction Designer

Zeyu Wang (b. 1997, Taiyuan, China) is a visual and interaction designer based in Milan, Italy.

Working across visual art, new media, and live performance, his practice is rooted in spatial aesthetics, with a particular focus on liminal conditions and perceptual thresholds. Through generative systems, machine learning, and interactive visual environments, he investigates how technology mediates spatial experience and destabilizes the boundaries between observation, presence, and interpretation. His interdisciplinary approach informs the visual direction of collaborative projects and immersive spatial interventions.

Altéra
Type
Live visual performance · Collaborative project
Date
Dec 2025–Apr 2026
Location
Milan, Italy
Role
Interaction & visual design
Visual collaborator
Giovanni Lo Castro (Visual concept & design, scenographic direction)

Altéra is a sonic and visual project presented at Circolo Amelia in Milan, born from the desire to cross thresholds between music and art, presence and absence, body and space. It is not a collective or an alias, but an environment: a place where musical research intertwines with visual gestures, installations, and ephemeral forms of contemporaneity.

Rooted in deep listening and ambient aesthetics, Altéra expands toward deconstructed electronics, broken rhythms, micro-textures, and hybrid forms between club culture and sound art. As resident visual artists, Zeyu Wang and Giovanni Lo Castro collaborate on the live visual direction and performance for Altéra's events, creating temporary audiovisual environments that respond to the sonic experience. Within this collaboration, Zeyu focuses on the development and interactive implementation of TouchDesigner-based real-time visual systems, while Giovanni contributes to the visual concept and scenographic direction. Together, their work extends Altéra's threshold-based language through live image composition and spatial perception.

Duna is an independent label and curatorial platform founded by Simone Scardino (Conceptual), bridging club culture with contemporary art. It treats experimental electronic music as a medium for critical exploration, fostering an evolving international network of underground artists.

Since 2025, Zeyu Wang and Giovanni Lo Castro have collaborated with Duna as visual artists, developing real-time visual systems for its events. Their work responds to the sonic, architectural, and spatial conditions of each venue, integrating live image-making with light, atmosphere, and collective presence.

Across multiple editions, the collaboration explores how moving images can operate as a temporary spatial layer, expanding the event into a multi-sensory environment shaped by sound, rhythm, and perception.

In-visible Gaze
Type
Live visual performance · Collaborative project
Date
Jul 10, 2025
Location
Milan, Italy
Role
Interaction & visual design
Visual collaborator
Giovanni Lo Castro (Direction, visual concept & design)

Conceived and directed by Giovanni Lo Castro and presented at Section 80 in Milan, In-visible Gaze is a live visual performance that translates clinical data into a profound sensory experience. The sonic landscape features a “sonification of the invisible” crafted by Iako and Novecento, utilizing voice and organic percussion to interpret the rhythmic flow of the images.

Acting as the interaction visual artist, I collaborated with Giovanni to translate this concept into a live deconstruction of machine vision. By employing hacked cameras and redirected sensors, we stripped surveillance devices of their traditional control functions, repurposing them into performative tools. This responsive visual environment exposes the audience to an automated vision that observes them without belonging to them, immersing the viewer in a sensing observatory where the act of seeing becomes a tactile and visceral experience.

Direction: Giovanni Lo Castro  ·  Sound: Iako & Novecento  ·  Visual: Zeyu Wang & Giovanni Lo Castro  ·  Curators: Benedetta Monteleone & Alessandro Quarta

PEEP BOX
Type
Interactive installation · Collaborative project
Date
Mar 2025
Location
Milan, Italy
Role
Concept, creative coding & installation design
Collaborator
Mehdi El Ouazzani (Fabrication & installation)

Created in collaboration with Mehdi El Ouazzani, PEEP BOX is an interactive installation that explores the opaque "black box" mechanisms of Artificial Intelligence. Inspired by pre-cinematic optical devices like the Kinetoscope, the work takes the form of a physical liminal tunnel. Inside, an automated AI system continuously translates real-time camera feeds into text descriptions on one end, and regenerates that text back into distorted images on the other.

Because viewers can only observe one screen at a time through designated windows, they are forced to physically move back and forth between the two ends to piece together the machine's internal computational logic. By placing the audience in a continuous loop of observing and being observed, PEEP BOX serves as a powerful spatial metaphor for the cognitive gap between human perception and rapid technological development.

Perfumen
Type
Mobile app design · Solo project
Date
2020
Location
Nanjing, China

Perfumen is an innovative mobile experience that bridges the gap between online convenience and offline sensory exploration in fragrance shopping. The app empowers users to intuitively blend their desired scent notes or even translate their uploaded photos into personalized fragrance profiles. To eliminate the uncertainty of online purchasing, Perfumen offers same-day delivery of custom concoction samples. Coupled with personalized gifting services like bespoke bottle engraving, it transforms perfume shopping into a seamless and highly customized journey.

Petradise
Type
Mobile app design · Solo project
Date
2020
Location
Nanjing, China

Petradise is a compassionate mobile ecosystem that bridges the gap between the finality of pet loss and the enduring bond of companionship. The app empowers users to coordinate bespoke memorial services and interact with AI-driven “digital twins” that preserve the essence of their departed pets. To support the healing journey, Petradise utilizes intelligent filtering to recommend adoption matches based on the original pet’s unique traits and history. Coupled with personalized ritual planning, it transforms the grieving process into a supportive and hope-filled bridge toward new beginnings.

15 Minutes
Type
Interactive installation · Solo project
Date
2020
Location
Nanjing, China

Drawing from Andy Warhol's prophecy of "15 minutes of fame," this interactive installation visualizes the ruthless and fleeting lifecycle of modern information consumption. Placed at a dining table—a daily site of digital consumption—the audience uses keyboard-like buttons to simulate online social behaviors. These actions trigger directed laser lights, representing the public gaze, camera lenses, and intense social discourse.

As the lasers focus on a suspended red helium balloon, the accumulated "attention" (heat) violently pops it, scattering its red sand to mark the climax of a viral event. Yet, this fame is short-lived; white sand continually pours from above, gradually and silently burying the red remnants. This physical process serves as a poetic metaphor for how new data relentlessly overwrites old information, shifting the audience's role from passive bystanders to active catalysts in the media machine.

Just as Marshall McLuhan famously posited that "the medium is an extension of man," this installation transforms our intangible digital interactions into a visceral physical reality. It reminds us that while we actively shape the media landscape, we are simultaneously consumed, monitored, and reshaped by it.

Section
Type
Interactive installation · Solo project
Date
2020
Location
Nanjing, China

Section is an interactive installation that explores the profound relationship between human presence, physical space, and the fluidity of time. By slicing and overlaying linear timelines, the work poetically restores different temporal moments into a singular spatial experience.

The installation centers around a circular projection initially displaying cinematic quotes about time. When a viewer approaches the projection to read, a Kinect sensor detects their presence: the text instantly vanishes, and the viewer's silhouette is silently recorded into the system's database, capturing them as a slice of the present moment to be met by future strangers.

The true cross-temporal encounter, however, emerges in absence. When the installation detects no human activity for over 15 seconds, the text dissolves, replaced by the blurred silhouettes of past visitors previously recorded by the system. These historical traces randomly combine and visually overlap, allowing strangers from different moments to poetically intersect within the exact same physical coordinates.

Lucus Angitiae
Type
Audiovisual performance · Collaborative project
Date
May 29, 2024
Location
Sansepolcro, Italy
Role
Visual design
Visual collaborator
Giovanni Lo Castro (Visual design)

Etere is a project by Giovanni Lo Castro that brings together contemporary art, digital technology, and traditional goldsmithing to explore forgotten mysticisms, folkloric traditions, and ancestral forms. Presented within the context of the Anti-Uroboros collection launch in Sansepolcro, Lucus Angitiae draws inspiration from the goddess Angitia and the Festa dei Serpari in Cocullo, Abruzzo. The project bridges esoteric symbolism and ancient goldsmith traditions, merging collective memory and nature into an audiovisual ritual.

As visual artists, Zeyu Wang and Giovanni Lo Castro designed the immersive projections, weaving archival documentary footage of the Festa dei Serpari into an evolving digital landscape to amplify the performance's ritualistic atmosphere.
